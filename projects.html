Patient Referral Management System

MIS 3371 capstone, University of Houston

August 2026 to present
Team of 5, in progress

The problem

Clinics send referrals by phone, fax, paper, and email. Coordinators re-type the details, chase missing information, and route referrals by hand, so referrals get lost, duplicates happen, and providers can't see the status.

The system

Our team built a transaction processing system for one transaction: a provider submits a patient referral for a service. The system checks the required fields, blocks duplicate active referrals, assigns a referral ID, and routes the referral to the right department. That department accepts it, rejects it with a reason, or asks for more information, and every status change is recorded with a timestamp.

What I do

  • I serve as the team's QA and Testing Lead.
  • Our documentation includes a 15-field data dictionary covering data types, validation rules, business rules, and system-generated fields.
  • I use GitHub for version control, reviewing code changes and coordinating with my team throughout development.

Where it stands

The front end (HTML, CSS, and JavaScript) is complete, with validation and business rules such as duplicate blocking and a review window that depends on urgency. A local Node.js backend passes 21 tests. All demo data is fictional.

Tools: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Node.js, GitHub, VS Code.

Hand-Scanner Placement Study

Amazon operations project

Summer 2025
Houston, Texas

The problem

Handheld scanners at work stations were often missing, broken, doubled up, or not charging. Associates left their stations to hunt for one, and productivity fell below the target rate.

What I did

  • Shadowed associates and managers to understand why scanners weren't being returned or charged.
  • Walked floors 2 to 5 and counted the scanners in each department, sorting them into correctly placed, missing, incorrectly placed, and doubled.
  • Found three causes: scanners put back in the wrong position so they never charged, stations left without a working scanner, and little accountability for returning equipment.
  • Estimated how much output is lost while an associate walks the floor looking for a scanner.

What I recommended

Only one department on the second floor had its scanners attached to a retractable tether. I recommended extending tethers to every station, starting with floors 2 to 5. I checked with the manufacturer and the maintenance team that tethers can be safely shortened to a length the facility chooses.

The goal was to keep scanners in place, make sure they are returned and charged, reduce downtime, and make it easy for leaders to hold people accountable. It is a low-cost fix with a high payoff.

Damaged and Dirty Tote Cleanup

Amazon operations project

Summer 2025
Houston, Texas

The problem

On every floor, damaged totes and "virtual dirty" totes (empty containers the system thinks still hold inventory) piled up in one area and took over floor space. The work is heavy, so it kept getting passed from one shift to the next.

What I did

  • Learned the process by talking with managers and process assistants from several teams.
  • Worked with a process assistant and two associates to clear the second floor, which held more than 30 pallets of totes. We moved them to an elevator, swapped them from plastic to wooden pallets, stacked and wrapped them, and loaded them for pickup.
  • Spotted the slowdowns: the nearest elevator was broken, so every load had to travel to one on the far side of the floor.
  • Set a goal with the process assistant to clear the whole facility before peak season. On the fourth floor I started with two tote runners and then three, and I split the job so one person carried totes halfway and the other took them down the elevator.

Results

  • Cleared the second floor in 3 days.
  • Cleared the second, fourth, and fifth floors in about 7 days. The third floor was under construction.

What I recommended

Instead of clearing piles in bulk, stop them from forming, starting with a checklist so that no one leaves tote problems for the next shift.
