PwC

Risk and Regulatory Internal Audit Intern

June 2026 to August 2026
San Francisco, California

The problem

A global technology client had 186 flagged software issues across several international markets. Each one had to be fixed, confirmed, and tracked so the client could show it met its regulatory requirements, including payment regulations, third-party risk, and governance.

What I did

  • I selected 19 of the 186 issues, each with a different type of problem, so I could see how a range of fixes worked.
  • For each one, I read the software engineer's code change to understand what went wrong and why, and I checked that the fix worked before the issue was closed.
  • I recorded every issue in a shared tracker and assessed control design and operating effectiveness to find control gaps.
  • I did the research that helped my senior team members prepare updates, and I built audit workpapers and client deliverables.
  • I built small Excel trackers to log my daily tasks and help keep each engagement project on schedule.

How it was delivered

I worked closely with a team of 5. I presented what I found to my engagement team, and then with my senior manager I updated the client. The client's feedback shaped how the remaining problems were handled.

What I took away

This was my introduction to consulting. I learned to turn technical detail into a clear update that a client can act on, and I saw how first, second, and third line defense work together on a real engagement.

Amazon

Area Manager Intern

June 2025 to August 2025
Houston, Texas

The problem

Each floor had to hit its target pick rate, and idle time and totes were pilling up and crowding flooes.

What I did

  • Analyzed real-time operational data to spot performance trends and decide how to schedule people.
  • Prioritized scheduling associates who could reach goal rates at certain times. Associates who couldn't were moved to a different department that worked with me on tote assessments.
  • Grouped pickers together so they could keep a steady pace, which raised the department's overall rate. I also built in short exercise breaks.
  • Used Lean and Six Sigma principles to improve pick-path efficiency and staffing workflows.
  • Audited scanner data across 5 floors and streamlined tote removal.
  • I would monitor floors walk my stations ensuring associates were not injured or having any robotic issues.

How I worked

I was an intern under an area manager. I watched how he assigned people on each floor, helped manage that workflow across all the floors, and got his approval before changes.

Results

  • Sustained 120% of the target pick rate.
  • Cut operational idle time by 12%.
  • Reduced tote processing time from 7 days to 3 days per floor.
